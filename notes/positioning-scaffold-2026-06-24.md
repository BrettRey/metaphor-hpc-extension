## The frame for the whole section

The existing paper engages the **cognitive-psychology** lineage of metaphor
(Gentner structure-mapping; Bowdle & Gentner career; Glucksberg categorization;
Holyoak & Stamenkovic multiconstraint; Murphy). It does **not** engage the
**cognitive-linguistics / metaphor-studies** lineage (Kövecses, Steen). For a
metaphor-studies venue that omission reads as not knowing the field. The fix is not
to concede novelty but to show the two named frameworks answer **different
questions** from HPC, so HPC neither competes with nor restates them.

One sentence that organizes the section:
> Extended CMT and DMT each add a dimension to CMT that is orthogonal to the one
> HPC adds. Kövecses adds *where a metaphor lives in a schematicity hierarchy and
> which context selects it online*; Steen adds *whether a metaphor is used as a
> metaphor in communication*; HPC adds *what maintains the source cluster across
> time and which mappings stay projectible*. None of the three reduces to another.

## Block A. Kövecses, Extended CMT (2020)

**Their claim (sourced).** Conceptual metaphors operate simultaneously on four
levels of schematicity: image schema -> domain -> frame -> mental space (plus the
linguistic level). The extended theory's headline move is **contextualist**:
speakers unconsciously select particular metaphors in online discourse under the
pressure of local and global contextual factors, with selection happening at the
mental-spaces level. It is built to fix CMT's neglect of context and its reliance
on dictionaries/corpora over "living" discourse. (eejpl review of Kövecses 2020;
confirm against the book.)

**What HPC adds that extended CMT doesn't.** Kövecses's "context" is an *online,
production-side, synchronic* selection pressure: which metaphor a speaker reaches
for here and now. HPC's "mechanism support" is a *world-side, diachronic* causal
process: what keeps the source cluster's properties co-occurring across generations,
and therefore which mappings persist, erode, or die. The review itself notes the
extended theory does not give an account of conventionalization or diachronic change.
That is precisely HPC's territory.

**Redescription rebuttal.** Three things HPC predicts that the four-level/contextual
apparatus does not:
1. *Erosion order.* When transmission weakens, peripheral (weakly maintained)
   members drop before core members (Dyirbal; English `data`). Schematicity levels
   and contextual selection say nothing about the *order* of decay.
2. *Conditioned conventionalization.* A high-frequency mapping conventionalizes only
   if the target domain has its own mechanisms sustaining the shared relational
   structure (Prediction 1). Contextual frequency-of-use is necessary, not
   sufficient. Kövecses's context predicts *use*, not *survival*.
3. *Cross-species recruitment.* The same operation runs in any animal with
   HPC-structured categories (the dog). Extended CMT is a theory of human
   conceptualization in discourse and makes no such prediction.

**Absorb, don't fight.** Kövecses's contextual factors are admissible to HPC as one
*mechanism type* (online contextual pressure on which metaphor surfaces). HPC's
claim is that selection-in-use sits on top of a stability layer Kövecses leaves
untheorized. Frame the relation as nested, not rival.

**Cultural variation hook (Kövecses 2005).** HPC already has a variation story: same
bodies, different metaphors, because the *maintaining mechanisms* (ecologies,
institutions, practices) differ across communities (this is already in §"Source
domains without mechanisms"). That directly answers the AE's "cultural variation"
item and connects to Kövecses's *Metaphor in Culture*. Variation tracks variation in
world-side mechanisms, not only context of use.

## Block B. Steen, Deliberate Metaphor Theory

**Their claim (sourced, via Flusberg et al. 2018).** DMT adds a *communicative*
dimension to the linguistic and conceptual dimensions: it distinguishes **deliberate**
metaphor (used intentionally as a metaphor, directing the addressee's attention to
the source domain) from **non-deliberate** metaphor (processed as part of ordinary
language, e.g. ARGUMENT IS WAR). The claimed payoff is cognitive consequences for the
listener. Flusberg notes critics find the central entailment (deliberate metaphors
grab more attention / are more memorable) poorly supported empirically
(Gibbs 2015; Thibodeau 2017). Cite Steen 2015, 2017.

**What HPC adds / how they relate.** DMT's axis is *communicative status*; HPC's axis
is *diachronic stability / projectibility*. They are orthogonal and cross-cut:

| | deliberate | non-deliberate |
|---|---|---|
| **high mechanism support** | Boyd's TCMs (scientist knowingly deploys "the brain is a computer" to direct research) | deeply conventionalized constitutive metaphors that have gone invisible |
| **low mechanism support** | literary metaphor (Dickinson deliberately deploys "hope is a bird") | dead/frozen idiom, no live projection |

The paper's own three-way typology (literary / everyday / constitutive, ordered by
mechanism support) is one axis of this table. DMT supplies a second, independent axis.
So HPC is not a rival to DMT and is not restating it.

**Divergent prediction.** Deliberateness and mechanism support dissociate. A metaphor
can be maximally deliberate (a fresh poetic coinage) yet have near-zero target-domain
mechanism support, and one can be wholly non-deliberate yet rest on heavy mechanism
support. So whatever processing signature DMT predicts from deliberateness, HPC
predicts a *separate* signature from mechanism support (graceful vs catastrophic
degradation under source-knowledge perturbation; conventionalization trajectory).
These can be measured independently. (Caution: don't lean on DMT's contested
attention/memory entailment; cite it as the dimension, not as settled result.)

## Block C. The concept-learning / analogy demand (AE charge #4)

**What's already there.** Gentner (structure-mapping), Bowdle & Gentner (career),
Holyoak & Stamenkovic (multiconstraint), Murphy (1996). The paper already treats
structure-mapping systematicity as "homeostatic coherence by another name" and folds
Holyoak's purpose constraint into projectibility.

**What's missing (the AE's "formal/computational concept learning").** The
theory-theory of conceptual coherence: Murphy & Medin 1985 — conceptual coherence
comes from background causal/explanatory theories, not from similarity. HPC is the
*world-side* completion of that move: coherence is maintained by causal mechanisms in
the world, and the learner's "theory" is projectible to the extent it tracks those
mechanisms. This is the cleanest one-paragraph add that satisfies the demand without
pretending to do a computational-modeling paper. Position HPC against similarity-based
models (prototype/exemplar) and representational structure-mapping by locating
cohesion in world-side maintenance rather than in the representation.

(Optional, venue-dependent: a sentence on Bayesian/structured concept-learning as the
formal cousin. Only if targeting Cognitive Science and only with a real source.)

## What this section does NOT need to become

Don't let it sprawl into a literature-review chapter. The move is surgical: for each
named framework, one paragraph that (a) states it fairly (Rapoport rule 1), (b) names
the different question it answers, (c) gives the HPC prediction it can't make. Three
tight paragraphs + the cross-cutting table beat a 5-page survey, and they read less
like an LLM than a survey would.
