# Metaphor and Symbol Desk Rejection - 2026-06-24

**Manuscript:** "What Dogs Know About Trails: Metaphor, Mechanisms, and the Phylogeny of Cross-Domain Extension"

**Journal:** *Metaphor and Symbol*

**Submission tracking ID previously recorded:** 260828053

**Decision:** Desk rejection / not sent for external review.

**Editor:** Herbert Colston

**Associate editor:** Stephen Flusberg

## Main Reasons Given

1. The manuscript is interesting and ambitious, but the associate editor did not see a sufficiently novel or specific contribution to metaphor theory.
2. The paper presents the HPC account as a significant update to Conceptual Metaphor Theory but does not engage deeply enough with adjacent metaphor-theory frameworks, especially:
   - Kövecses's extended conceptual metaphor theory;
   - Steen's deliberate metaphor theory;
   - metaphor in discourse across languages;
   - work on cultural variation, context sensitivity, and conventionalization.
3. Core terms need sharper specification: mechanisms, mechanism support, projectibility, functional domains, and cross-domain extension.
4. A revised version should engage formal/computational approaches to concept learning, category structure, metaphor, and analogy.
5. The prose was seen as polished but generic in an LLM-associated way: broad claims, repeated argument structures, repeated grammatical constructions, and fast transitions across complex theory.

## Diagnosis

This is not a rejection of the core idea as uninteresting. It is a venue-fit and execution rejection. For *Metaphor and Symbol*, the paper has to prove it is doing something inside current metaphor theory, not only using metaphor as a domain where projectibility-profile machinery is interesting.

## Revision Implications

If retargeting to another metaphor venue, the paper needs a major theory-facing rewrite:

- establish the gap against extended CMT, deliberate metaphor theory, discourse/cross-linguistic metaphor work, analogy, and computational concept-learning literature;
- define the contribution as a specific mechanism/profile account of cross-domain extension, not as a broad HPC reframing of CMT;
- replace generic transitions and repeated argument skeletons with source-anchored local moves;
- sharpen the novelty claim around what the account predicts or disqualifies that existing metaphor frameworks do not.

If retargeting outside metaphor theory, preserve the animal-cognition / philosophy-of-science spine and avoid promising a major update to CMT. Better fit may be a philosophy-of-cognitive-science or cognitive-ethology venue after the LLM-prose and term-specification issues are fixed.

## Next Action

Do not resubmit this version. Choose between:

1. a major metaphor-theory rewrite; or
2. a retargeted philosophy/cognitive-science version with a narrower claim about cross-domain extension and projectibility.
