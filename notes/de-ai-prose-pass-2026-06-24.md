# De-AI prose pass: located tic inventory

Built 2026-06-24 in response to M&S desk-reject charge #3 ("polished but generic
LLM prose, repeated argument structures and grammatical constructions, transitions
that move too fast over complex theory"). These are STRUCTURAL tics a sentence-level
linter misses. Line numbers are post-deep-alignment main.tex.

This is a location map for Brett's voice pass, not a request to auto-rewrite. The
prose is mostly Claude-drafted (see Acknowledgements), which is what the AE detected.
The structural fixes below are mechanical and safe; making it read as Brett's own
voice is the irreducibly human part.

## Priority 1 — the double enumeration (lines 162–168)

The worst structural offender: two consecutive "adds N things / First… Second…
Third…" lists. Dissolve the scaffold; let the points flow as connected argument.

WORKED DEMONSTRATION (shows the structural fix — dissolving "First/Second/Third" —
not a claim that it now reads as Brett's voice):

> Three gains follow for metaphor theory. Lakoff and Johnson describe the property
> clusters; the account says why they cohere and why some properties sit closer to
> the core, since metaphors persist only while the clusters they map between keep
> their support, and they fray from the edges inward when that support weakens (as
> Dyirbal radial categories do once transmission falters). The same machinery buys
> the falsifiability Lakoff's ICMs lack: a profile claim fails wherever no support
> can be identified or the cluster won't project to new cases, so a metaphor whose
> shared structure is unsupported in the target is, on this account, demonstrably
> superficial rather than merely unfashionable. And prototype effects stop being
> illusions: an instance carrying few of a cluster's properties is less typical than
> one carrying many because what comes in degrees is stability, not membership.

Then the "Metaphor adds two things… First… Second…" paragraph (168) gets the same
treatment: fold cross-domain extension and literal/metaphorical gradience into prose
without the "First/Second" frame.

## Priority 2 — the "right/wrong" cadence (lines 60, 176)

Two paragraphs run the same construction. Line 60: "Lakoff was right that… He was
right that… He was right that…". Line 176: "Lakoff was right that… He was wrong
that… Boyd was right that… He was wrong to…". Keep at most one; rephrase the other
so the concession isn't a drumbeat. (The point in 176 — embodiment is one stabilizer
grade among several — can be stated directly without the right/wrong scaffolding.)

## Priority 3 — paragraph openers that name the object (14 paragraphs)

Lines 39, 45, 64, 112, 162, 170, 172, 180, 201, 203, 205, 280, 317 open by placing
the argumentative object on the table ("The framework…", "The reframing…", "The
gap…", "The divergence…", "The picture that emerges is this."). Per house style this
is distributional, not banned: thin it so fewer than half of section-internal
paragraphs open this way. Easiest wins — rewrite the openers at 180 ("The gap can
now be stated precisely"), 205 ("This gives a three-way typology"), 280 ("The
picture that emerges is this"), 317 ("The reframing developed here has implications
in both directions").

## Priority 4 — meta-narration / throat-clearing openers

- 139 "With both frameworks on the table, the reframing is straightforward." → just
  state the reframing.
- 180 "The gap can now be stated precisely." → state it.
- 323 "The testability asymmetry is worth emphasizing." → emphasize it by stating it.
- 132 "There's a reflexive point here worth pausing on." → make the point.
- 266 "A caveat is needed here." → give the caveat.

## What NOT to do
- Don't overcorrect into the trailing "-ing" tic ("…, leading to", "…, allowing").
- Don't replace one cadence with another uniform one. Vary sentence openings.
- Keep the source-anchored local moves; the problem is the connective scaffolding
  between them, not the grounded claims.
