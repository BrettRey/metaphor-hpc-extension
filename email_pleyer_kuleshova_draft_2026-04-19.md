Dear Michael and Svetlana,

I just read your *Cognitive Science* paper on the evolution of analogy, and the two moves that stuck with me were the productional-diversity method for reading analogical capacities off the archaeological record and the explicit anti-Rubicon framing. I've been working on two papers that converge on your phylogeny-of-analogy argument from a different direction, and they might be of use to you.

The first (*What dogs know about trails: Metaphor, mechanisms, and the phylogeny of cross-domain extension*, currently under review at *Metaphor and Symbol*, PsyArXiv preprint https://osf.io/preprints/psyarxiv/vuafy_v1) argues that dogs systematically extend their ecological TRAIL category to novel instances (painted lines on soccer fields, road markings) on the basis of partial property overlap rather than full perceptual similarity. If that reading is right, it's a non-hominin living case of the graded cross-domain mapping you document, sitting between the corvid/primate tool-use evidence and the comparative-metaphor work on magnitude-space mappings. It speaks directly to the second open question you flag in your conclusion about how exactly human analogical capacities differ from nonhumans: the answer may be quantitative rather than qualitative, and dogs give a clean test case.

The second (*Noticing language: What echolocation tells us about language emergence*, under review at *Evolutionary Linguistic Theory*, LingBuzz https://ling.auf.net/lingbuzz/009302) is a language-evolution piece aimed at your broader research programme at the Center. It argues that human echolocation — a genuinely novel cognitive-perceptual capacity acquired by blind users in adulthood — reveals something about what sort of capacity language emergence actually requires. The evolutionary-continuity thread connects.

Both papers draw on Boyd's HPC framework, applying it to cognitive categories rather than the biological-species cases it originated in. Your productional-diversity method and the HPC stabilising-mechanisms picture have complementary jobs: yours infers the cognitive capacity from the material record; HPC tells you what makes the cluster hold together once the capacity is there. I'd be curious whether any of it is useful to what you're working on next.
